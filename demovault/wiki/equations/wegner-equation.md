## Statement

The Wegner equation governing the evolution of the probability distribution $p_t(\phi) = e^{-S_t(\phi)}$ under the PIK flow is:

$$\frac{dp_t(\phi)}{dt} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left[\dot{\phi}_t(\phi)\, p_t(\phi)\right]$$

For the PIRG pair $(S_t, \dot{\phi}_t)$ with $p_t = e^{-S_t}$, this becomes the PDE for the physics-informed kernel $\dot{\phi}_t$:

$$\frac{dS_t(\phi)}{dt} + \dot{\phi}_t(\phi)\frac{\partial S_t(\phi)}{\partial\phi} = \frac{\partial\dot{\phi}_t(\phi)}{\partial\phi}$$

This is a linear first-order PDE for $\dot{\phi}_t$ given $S_t$.

## Variables

- $p_t(\phi) = e^{-S_t(\phi)}$: probability distribution at RG-time $t \in [0,1]$
- $\dot{\phi}_t(\phi)$: physics-informed kernel — the infinitesimal map generated by the flow
- $S_t(\phi)$: action at RG-time $t$ (analytically specified)
- $\frac{dS_t}{dt}$: total RG-time derivative of the action
- $\frac{\partial S_t}{\partial\phi}$: field derivative of the action (force term)
- $\frac{\partial\dot{\phi}_t}{\partial\phi}$: divergence of the kernel (Jacobian source term)

## Derivation sketch

Start from the continuity equation (conservation of probability weight) in the space of distributions: $\frac{d}{dt}\int_\mathcal{M} d\mu_t\, p_t(\phi) = 0$. For a flow $\phi \to \phi + \dot{\phi}_t\, dt$ with measure $d\mu_t$, applying the chain rule and requiring the identity to hold for all sub-manifolds gives the Wegner form. The action form follows by writing $p_t = e^{-S_t}$ and differentiating.

## Limits and special cases

- Fokker-Planck limit: $\dot{\phi}_t \propto \partial S_t/\partial\phi$ recovers the Langevin/Fokker-Planck equation with classical action $S_t$.
- Polchinski equation: specific choice of $S_t$ recovers the Polchinski flow for the Wilsonian effective action.
- Harmonic oscillator: the kernel is purely analytic, $\dot{\phi}_t = -\frac{1}{2}M_t^{-1}\frac{dM_t}{dt}\phi$, with $M_t$ the time-dependent mass matrix.
- Difference of Wegner equations at two field values $\phi$ and $\chi$ eliminates the unknown normalisation term $d\log\mathcal{N}_t/dt$, giving a fully computable condition for $\dot{\phi}_t$.
